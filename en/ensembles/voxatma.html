Why this name?

Rediscovering what was already there.

Atma, or Ātman, is a Sanskrit word that literally means “essence, breath” — the language itself links, at its root, the act of breathing to the innermost self. In Indian philosophy, Ātman names what remains once we strip away the noise around us: essence, the inner self. That's exactly where VoxAtma begins. Breathe. Listen. Sing. For some, this is a first choral experience. For others, it's returning to music after ten, twenty or thirty years. For everyone, it's a space where, for a while, the pace of daily life is left at the door. We work on vocal technique, breathing, repertoire, listening and building a collective sound — but there's another transformation too: the one that happens when a person hears their own voice again.

VoxLaci's adult choir in Cascais, with or without musical experience. Breathing, voice and repertoire on Monday evenings.

VoxAtma welcomes adults with or without musical experience. It is a space to develop the voice, sing varied repertoire, reduce stress and transform individual experience through regular artistic practice. As across VoxLaci, being an amateur here carries the word's oldest sense: someone who loves what they do.
